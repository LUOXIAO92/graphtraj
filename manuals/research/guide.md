# Research

State the question, scope and the evidence needed to answer it. Read available
material and previous findings before collecting more. Decide which unknowns
would change the conclusion; focus effort there.

Choose sources appropriate to the question: original documents, papers, data,
firsthand records or direct observations. Follow important secondary claims
to their primary evidence where possible. Record dates, versions and context
when they affect applicability. An inaccessible source is a limitation, not
permission to imply it was inspected.

Separate what the evidence establishes from inference and speculation. Compare
conflicting findings, check the scope of each claim, and state what remains
unknown. A lack of discovered evidence does not by itself prove absence. Stop
when the evidence answers the bounded question, or describe the specific gap
that available sources and resources cannot resolve.

Investigate directly, or delegate bounded independent questions when authorized
and useful with the available resources. Give each assignment its question,
source constraints and expected evidence, without prescribing its conclusion.
No particular model, team composition or background Agent is required.

Deliver an answer with citations beside material claims, relevant artifacts,
limitations and any next investigation justified by unresolved uncertainty.
Use the project's existing result location and required format. Preserve
provenance without copying source material unnecessarily. Follow the assigned
writing authority rather than requiring a particular role or new report system.
